Twelve Pages, Nine Thousand Words — And No Title On Your Homepage.

You have never worked with a marketing team, and the honest news is that you do not need much done.

You publish twelve pages and 9,063 words, including separate pages for commercial roofing, new construction, recover and replacement, repairs and service areas. That is a genuinely good structure.

But your homepage has no title tag at all, which is the single most important line of text on any website.

Every page read individually, 25 September 2026
Finding 1 — Your Homepage Has No Title
<title> empty </title>
This is the line Google prints as the blue clickable link

The title tag is what appears as the heading of your result in search, and what shows on a browser tab. Yours is empty.

When a title is missing, Google writes its own — usually by grabbing whatever text it finds first on the page. So the most prominent line about your business in every search result is written by a machine rather than by you.

It is also one of the strongest signals for what a page should rank for. Yours currently says nothing about commercial roofing, nothing about Northville, and nothing about Michigan.

This is a ten-minute fix and it is the highest-return item in this document.

Finding 2 — No Structured Data At All
RoofingContractor / LocalBusiness not found Service markup not found FAQ markup not found Rating markup not found Analytics: Google Analytics 4 G-N766H20C33 present Google Tag Manager GTM-MCQT3WF2 present Meta pixel not found (we opened the container and checked)
The measurement is there. The description is not.

Structured data is the code that tells Google what kind of business you are, where you are and what you do. Without it, Google has to guess from your words alone — and your title tag is empty, so it has less to go on than it should.

You do have Google Analytics and Tag Manager running, which puts you ahead of most contractors. We fetched your tag manager container directly and confirmed there is no Meta pixel inside it — but the framework is installed, so adding one is minutes rather than a project.

Finding 3 — Your Reviews Page Is 117 Words
/ 1,219 words /service-areas 1,211 /contact-us 941 /commercial-roofing 932 /about 879 /roof-repairs 851 /roof-recover-replacements 792 /gallery 765 /new-construction-roofing 722 /blog 428 /referrals 206 /reviews 117 TOTAL: 12 pages, 9,063 words
On commercial work, proof is the product

Everything on your site is solidly written except the two pages that prove other people trust you. Reviews at 117 words and referrals at 206.

You told River you have just finished a job that ran two weeks and you can take on anything, large or small. A two-week commercial job is exactly the kind of work a prospective client wants evidence of — and there is almost nothing published about any job you have done.

Your gallery has 765 words, which is better. But a building owner choosing a roofer wants to read what the problem was, what you did and what it cost to put right.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Write the title tag. Ten minutes. It is the line Google prints in every search result and it is currently blank.
  • Add RoofingContractor and Service structured data, so Google is told what you are rather than left to infer it.
  • Write up your jobs. The two-week job you just finished is a case study. What the building was, what was wrong, what you did. That is what commercial buyers read.
  • Rebuild the reviews page. 117 words is not enough to reassure anybody spending five figures on a roof.
  • Add the Meta pixel through the tag manager you already have.
  • Then look at getting in front of more buildings — once what they find when they check you is working properly.
