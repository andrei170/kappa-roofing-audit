Finding 1 — The Gap Nobody In This Market Has Filled
Firms checked for a named project, case study or portfolio page: 4 Firms publishing one: 0
This is the finding worth the most and it costs nothing but your time

You told River you have just finished a two-week job. Nobody in this comparison has published anything like it. Not the firm with thirty pages, not the firm in Ann Arbor, not you.

Commercial roofing is not bought the way domestic roofing is bought. A homeowner picks on price and how quickly you answer. A building owner or a property manager is spending a lot of somebody else's money and has to justify the choice to someone above them. What they want is evidence you have done this exact thing before, on a building like theirs.

Three or four written-up jobs would make you the only firm in this comparison offering that. Each one needs: what the building was, what was wrong, what you specified and why, how long it took, and a photograph. You already have all of that in your head and on your phone.

Two items, both cheap, and one of them tells you what they are spending on

A Meta pixel on a commercial roofing site means somebody is running or preparing to run paid social. You have Tag Manager already installed, so adding yours is minutes of work rather than a project — and until it is there, you cannot retarget anyone who visits your site.

FAQ markup is the other one. It is what makes Google show a list of expandable questions under a result instead of one plain line. It takes up more of the screen and pushes competitors down. You have four properly written service pages that would carry it easily.

Neither of these is a rebuild. Both sit on top of what you already have.

Finding 3 — New Roof Publishes 25 Pages And Measures None Of Them
New Roof, Inc. (newroof.com) Pages 25 Analytics none found Tag Manager none found Meta pixel none found LocalBusiness none found Tappable numbers 4 Positioning Ann Arbor, and roofing/siding/windows rather than commercial
They are building volume with no idea what any of it does

They publish a page for each service in each town — ventilation, chimney repair, windows, roughly 1,500 words each. That is real effort. But with no analytics of any kind, they cannot tell which of those pages has ever produced a phone call.

They are also not really your competitor and that matters. Their title says "Roofing, Siding & Windows in Ann Arbor" — they are a domestic contractor selling three trades. You are commercial, and your site says so clearly. That separation is worth more than it looks: when a facilities manager lands on your site, they know within a line that you do their kind of work.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Write up three jobs, starting with the two-week one. Nobody in this market has done it. It is the only item here that no amount of competitor spending can copy quickly, because they would need the jobs first.
  • Add the Meta pixel through the Tag Manager you already have. Minutes. Roof Management has one.
  • Add call tracking. On commercial work most enquiries are phone calls, and right now you cannot see them. This is the single biggest blind spot in your setup, and it is not a competitor issue — it is yours.
  • Add FAQ markup to the four service pages. Roof Management has it, you do not, and your pages are already written well enough to carry it.
  • Fix the reviews page. At 117 words it is the thinnest thing on your site, and on commercial work it is doing the same job as the case studies you have not written.
  • Do not buy more pages. New Roof has twice your page count and cannot measure a single one of them. Your twelve pages are better written than most of what we read today.

You are second best-equipped in this market, and the best ground in it is unclaimed.

Nobody here publishes proof of work. You have just finished a two-week job. That is the whole opportunity.
